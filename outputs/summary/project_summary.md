# Project Summary

## Research Question

Is the proportion of current cigarette use different between male and female students?

---

## Main Analysis: Gender and Current Cigarette Use

### Dataset Summary

| Group | Sample Size | Current Smokers | Smoking Proportion |
|---------|---------:|---------:|---------:|
| Female | 6,740 | 1,167 | 17.3% |
| Male | 6,572 | 1,418 | 21.6% |

Total Sample Size: 13,312 students

### Statistical Method

- Two-Proportion z-Test
- 95% Confidence Interval for Difference in Proportions
- Significance Level: α = 0.05

### Results

| Item | Value |
|---------|---------|
| Difference (Male − Female) | 0.0426 |
| z Statistic | 6.215 |
| p-value | < 0.001 |
| 95% Confidence Interval | (0.029, 0.056) |

### Interpretation

Male students showed a significantly higher proportion of current cigarette use than female students.

The estimated smoking proportion was 21.6% among male students and 17.3% among female students. The observed difference was approximately 4.3 percentage points.

Because the p-value was smaller than 0.001 and the confidence interval did not include zero, the difference was considered statistically significant.

## Extension 1: Female Smoker Behavioral Profile

### Research Question

Do female smokers exhibit different behavioral and psychological characteristics compared with female non-smokers?

### Group Summary

| Group | Sample Size |
|---------|---------:|
| Female Non-Smokers | 5,573 |
| Female Smokers | 1,463 |

### Behavioral Comparison

| Variable | Non-Smoker | Smoker |
|---------|---------:|---------:|
| Alcohol Use | 1.55 | 2.90 |
| Binge Drinking | 1.28 | 2.44 |
| Marijuana Use | 1.16 | 2.22 |
| Physical Fighting | 1.44 | 1.99 |
| Sleep | 3.83 | 3.39 |

### Statistical Results

All Mann–Whitney U tests produced:

- p-value < 0.001

SadOrHopeless analysis:

| Test | Result |
|---------|---------|
| Chi-square Statistic | 214.61 |
| p-value | < 0.001 |

### Interpretation

Female smokers reported:

- Higher alcohol use
- Higher binge drinking frequency
- Higher marijuana use
- Higher levels of physical fighting
- Higher prevalence of sadness or hopelessness

Female smokers also reported lower sleep scores, suggesting shorter sleep duration.

## Extension 2: Smoking Intensity Trend Analysis Among Female Students

Do behavioral risk characteristics become more prevalent as smoking intensity increases?

|---------|---------:|

### Chi-Square Results

| Variable | Chi-Square | p-value |
|---------|---------:|---------|

| Marijuana Use | 1981.21 | <0.001 |

### Interpretation

Higher smoking intensity was associated with:

- Higher alcohol use
- Higher binge drinking frequency
- Higher marijuana use
- Higher levels of physical fighting
- Higher prevalence of sadness or hopelessness

Marijuana use showed the strongest increasing trend across smoking intensity groups.

Sleep duration showed an inverse relationship with smoking intensity, with heavier smokers reporting shorter sleep duration.

---

## Overall Conclusion

The Cycle 3 analysis demonstrates that smoking behavior is associated with broader behavioral and psychological risk factors among students.

The main analysis showed that male students were significantly more likely to be current cigarette users than female students.

Among female students, smokers exhibited higher levels of substance use, physical fighting, and sadness or hopelessness than non-smokers.

Furthermore, increasing smoking intensity was associated with increasingly elevated levels of multiple behavioral risk characteristics.

These findings suggest that smoking behavior is part of a broader behavioral risk profile rather than an isolated health behavior.

Because the YRBS 2007 dataset is observational survey data, all findings should be interpreted as associations rather than causal relationships.
